# Data in motion, and maths and science as code

Two families that live entirely in the code layer: numbers and charts, and equations,
geometry and simulations. Both fail the same way when generated by a diffusion model
(wrong digits, garbled labels, unstable geometry), so both are drawn by code. Evidence
tags: V verified (primary source or local test), R reported, I inference.

## Chart honesty

1. Bars and columns start at zero. A truncated bar axis is deceptive; people still
   overestimate the difference when told the axis is truncated. When differences are small,
   change the chart (dot plot, range plot, plot the differences), not the baseline.
2. Line charts need not start at zero but extend to zero when values come near it; for shares
   near 100% extend to 100%.
3. Do not scale icons in two dimensions: area is perceived at about the 0.7 power of its
   size (2x area looks like 1.6x, 10x looks like 5x). Use repeated equal icons or one
   dimension. No 3D, no decorative dimensions.
4. Label units with the first appearance of the number, label every mark, add a source line.
5. Every animation frame is a valid data graphic; never interpolate through values that do not
   exist. The axis never rescales mid-animation.
6. Assert on data, not pixels: for each bar, `rendered_height / max_height` equals
   `value / max_value` within 0.02 at the hold frame, and the baseline sits on the zero line.

## Chart type versus claim

| Claim | Best | In 20 to 90 s | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| comparison or magnitude | ordered bars from zero, dot plot | 2 to 5 bars, one highlighted, values printed on bars | pie, bubble, unlabelled area |
| change over time | line (1 hero plus grey context), slope chart for 2 points | one line draws at a time, 2 to 3 lines, endpoint label | more than 3 coloured lines; dual axes |
| part to whole | stacked bar, 100% bar, waffle | pie or donut only for 2 to 3 slices | pies with many or similar slices |
| ranking | ordered bar, lollipop from zero | top 5 to 8 rows, stable colour per entity | unsorted bars; races with constant swaps |
| distribution | dot strip, histogram | one annotated outlier | box plots for general audiences |
| relationship | scatter | 10 to 25 points, 1 to 3 labelled | dense unlabelled scatter |
| deviation | diverging bar | prominent zero line | rainbow scales |
| spatial | choropleth for rates and ratios, proportional symbols for counts | one highlighted region plus context | choropleth of raw counts |
| flow | sankey, waterfall | 3 to 5 nodes | unlabelled ribbons |

## How much one beat can carry

Heuristics derived from reading speed and static-chart maxima (I): bars 5 (7 for a ranking
with a top-3 highlight); ranking rows 8 in portrait; line series 3; stacked segments 3 to 4
per stack and 3 stacks; pie slices 3; scatter points 25 with 1 to 3 labelled; map one
highlighted region; distinct data colours 3 plus grey; text items on a chart beyond the
title 7; annotations one hero callout plus at most one context. Hold budget:
`build (1.0 to 1.5 s) + 0.4 s per label the viewer must read + 1.0 s takeaway dwell`. A
3-bar comparison with 3 values is about 3.4 s; a 5-row ranking about 4.2 s. Three to five
numbers per beat matches working-memory limits.

## Animating charts

Evidence: animated transitions beat static for tracking and estimation, staged animation is
preferred, but heavy staging raises error, and about 1 s per stage is recommended. An animated
trend was the fastest way to present a trend and the slowest and least accurate for the viewer
to analyse many series. So animate one narrated trend or comparison; for multi-series
comparison show a static overlay with one highlighted series, or small multiples, and narrate.

Sequence inside a chart beat: baseline, axis and title first (0.2 to 0.4 s); marks grow
(0.7 to 1.0 s); value labels land as each mark lands; the hero is emphasised and the rest
fade to 30 to 40%; the annotation lands on the voice word; hold. Marks grow from their
baseline; lines draw left to right (right to left for RTL languages) at constant speed tied to
the voice; pies sweep clockwise from 12 o'clock. Same data means the same mark across beats:
re-sort by position swap (0.4 to 0.6 s, `power2.inOut`), not fade-out and fade-in. At most two
stages per change (axis or scale, then values).

Bar-chart race (R, thin evidence): at most 8 rows in portrait, a fixed colour and label per
entity, value at the bar end in tabular figures, a period counter of at least 120 px, about
one rank swap per second at most, swaps 0.4 to 0.6 s, the whole race at most 20 s, then freeze
on the final frame and annotate the one fact the video is about. Skip a race whose story is
only the final winner.

## Numbers, scale and maps

**Count-ups.** 1.0 to 1.6 s with `expo.out` or `power3.out`; hold at least 0.8 to 1.0 s after
the last digit lands (the eye locks on the near-final number early, so the hold matters more
than the count length). Count from the previous value when the story is change; from zero
only for a total that builds. Round to the precision the claim needs. Unit and prefix are
static, only digits move. Fixed-width digits are mandatory (`04-style-bible.md`). Rolling
digits: one vertical 0 to 9 strip per position in a clipped cell whose height equals the line
box; ones roll fastest. Sound: a single end tick or a rising riser, never a tick per integer.

**Counter plus context.** Big number lands and holds 1 s; a reference object lands next to it
at the same scale; the ratio label ("3.2x"); one line of meaning. One comparison per number.
Show the reference's own value so the ratio is checkable. Time scale facts: 1 million seconds
is 11.57 days; 1 billion seconds is 31.69 years. Compare like with like, to something the
audience has seen (an area overlaid on a map they know, not football fields).

**Scale tricks, in order of cleanliness:** shared baseline and scale (bars); a powers-of-ten
zoom with a ruler ("each step is 10x"); an overlay on a familiar object or map (say "about");
repeated unit icons; conversion to human time or money. Avoid circle area for the headline
comparison and log axes for general audiences.

**Maps.** No projection is right, only better or worse for a purpose. Mercator inflates area
by sec squared of latitude (x2.0 at 45 degrees, x4.0 at 60, x8.6 at 70). For a world choropleth
or any size comparison use an equal-area projection (Equal Earth, `d3.geoEqualEarth`); city
and region zooms can use Web Mercator; long routes use an orthographic globe; do not switch
projection between consecutive beats unless the cut is the point. Choropleth for rates,
proportional symbols for counts. Camera: one continuous pan and zoom path between highlights,
labels riding the camera, `power2.inOut`, 1.2 to 2.0 s; highlight by fill and dim the rest to
30 to 40%; labels appear after the camera settles; one highlighted region per beat; borders at
least 2 px; labels at least 40 px. Route: pre-project GeoJSON with d3-geo to SVG paths and
animate with a seekable GSAP timeline (a D3 transition is not seekable in HyperFrames).
Disputed borders and names: choose one stated rule per project and apply it everywhere.

**Timelines.** Time runs top to bottom in portrait; a cursor draws the line with the voice;
events appear as it passes, labels landing on the voice word; at most 5 events on screen,
longer timelines pan; a large current-date marker (at least 56 px, tabular); show scale
breaks; keep the line the same object across beats.

## Annotation and element specs

Highlight the one thing: all marks neutral grey for 0.4 to 0.6 s so the viewer sees the whole;
the hero takes the accent and the rest drop to 30 to 40% opacity (0.3 to 0.4 s); a callout line
and label land on the voice word; hold for the reading time. One highlight hue; never add a new
hue for de-emphasised data.

| Element at 1080x1920 | Minimum | Preferred |
|---|---|---|
| category or axis label | 40 px | 44 px medium or bold |
| value label | 44 px bold | 56 px |
| source or unit line | 36 px | 40 px at 4.5:1 or better |
| horizontal bar thickness | 70 px | 80 to 110 px, gap at least 24 px |
| vertical column width | 120 px | 160 to 240 px |
| baseline stroke | 4 px | 6 px |
| gridlines | none | 2 px at 15 to 20%, at most 3 |
| line stroke | hero 8 px, context 4 to 5 px | |
| marker dots | 24 px | 32 px |
| mark contrast with background | 3:1 | 4.5:1 |
| rows in a ranking | 8 | 5 to 6 |

Thin strokes shimmer or disappear after platform recompression (I).

## Maths and science: engine routing

| Need | Choose |
|---|---|
| derivations with term-level morphing, geometric proofs, function transformations, graphs with moving tangents, vector fields, `ValueTracker` coupling | Manim Community Edition |
| hook text, big numbers, stat cards, comparison cards, UI-style diagrams, captions, brand frames, lower thirds, simple charts, anything needing text wrapping or brand fonts | HTML via HyperFrames (KaTeX for static or lightly animated equations) |
| both in one short | render the one maths stage in Manim with a transparent overlay (`--transparent --format mov` gave QTRLE with alpha) or as a clip, and compose it into an HTML timeline that owns type, captions and brand; share one colour map |
| organic or photographic content | not code: stock, real or AI |

Manim Community Edition for unattended agents: it renders on CPU with no display and has a
stable CLI. ManimGL depends on an interactive OpenGL workflow and is not recommended unattended.
HTML wins on typography, numbers and layout checks: HyperFrames' own `check` flags equations
wider than the frame, where Manim needs a custom audit. HTML has no built-in equation-to-equation
morph; Motion Canvas has part-based LaTeX morphing but its headless rendering is unconfirmed.
KaTeX needs `trust: true` for per-term colour classes; bundle fonts so renders stay offline and
deterministic.

## Vertical Manim contract

Verified on Manim CE 0.22.0.

- `-r 1080,1920` alone letterboxes: pixels become 1080x1920 but the frame stays 14.22 x 8 units, so the
  scene shrinks into a band. Set all four in the script: `config.pixel_width = 1080`,
  `config.pixel_height = 1920`, `config.frame_width = 9`, `config.frame_height = 16` (1 unit is 120 px).
  Draft at 540x960 and 15 fps; deliver 1080x1920 at 30 fps; 60 fps rarely helps a short.
- Defaults ignore the new frame: `Axes()` is still 12 units wide, `NumberPlane()` landscape-sized.
  Pass explicit sizes, for example `Axes(x_length=7.5, y_length=7.5)`.
- `Text` never wraps. At the default size 48 only about 24 characters fit a line (cap height 58 px).
  Author every line break, 4 to 8 words per line, 2 lines. Body at 48 and labels at 36 or larger; the
  16:9 scale in popular skills (body 30, labels 24) is too small here. Proportional sans for text,
  LaTeX for maths, mono only for code (mono is wider and clips at the frame edge). Pin a font you
  ship: a missing font falls back silently.
- Counters, tick labels and `BarChart` need LaTeX; preflight `latex --version` and `dvisvgm --version`
  and render one `MathTex(r"E=mc^2")` still. Use raw strings for every `MathTex`.
- Zones on the 9x16 frame (y from +8 to -8): hook or question top 14%; a square 8x8 stage for the one
  diagram; the equation or big number below it on one line (`set_width(min(w, 8))`); the bottom 17% is
  reserved for captions and platform UI (use the measured platform bands in `10-platforms.md` as the
  hard limit). Stack comparisons top and bottom, never side by side.
- Beat structure for 20 to 90 s: hook 0 to 3 s with the object already moving and the question in 4 to 8
  words (no title card); setup 3 to 10 s with one concrete case and the colour map assigned; 3 to 8
  build beats of 6 to 10 s (change 1 to 1.5 s, read 0.5 to 1 s, point 0.5 s, one sentence of
  narration); payoff with a 1 s breath before it and the longest hold (3 to 4 s) after it.
- Spoken pace 130 to 150 wpm for deliberate maths narration; a 60 s short is 6 to 8 beats and about
  150 to 200 words.
- A one-line equation wider than about 8 units must be split or scaled. Break across lines aligned on
  the equals sign and animate term by term in the order of understanding: show the whole equation at
  0.2 opacity, then light terms in turn.

## Timing and verification

Audio first: generate the voice-over per line, measure real durations with `ffprobe`, then build scenes
to those durations. Never set scene timings before the voice is measured. Offset visuals about 0.2 to
0.4 s early so the picture leads the word. No loudness processing on generated audio.

Manim frame rounding drifts. `play(run_time=t)` rounds up to whole frames and `wait(t)` rounds down, with
a one-frame minimum. At 15 fps `play(0.37)` takes 0.400 s and `wait(0.37)` 0.333 s; 40 plays of 0.37 s
(14.8 s) rendered 16.0 s, and a subtitle linter that sums nominal run times passed it. Use run times that
are multiples of 1/fps and close each segment against a scene-clock target:
`frames = round((t_target - self.renderer.time) * fps); self.wait(frames / fps + 0.25 / fps)`
(landed exactly on 26.000 s at 15, 30 and 60 fps on CE 0.22.0; re-verify on your version).

Physics: explicit Euler inside an updater is frame-rate dependent and unphysical (a pendulum ended at 4.1x,
2.7x and 1.8x its starting energy at 15, 30 and 60 fps). Precompute the trajectory with RK4 at a fine
step and index it with a `ValueTracker` clock. Seed anything random (`--seed` exists since CE 0.20).

Verification ladder, in order: preflight (LaTeX, fonts); last-frame still (`manim -ql -s`); a geometry
audit that checks every mobject's bounds against the safe rectangle after each `play` and `wait` (it
misses mid-animation overflow); contact sheet; `freezedetect` with a very low noise floor (`n=0.000005`:
`n=0.0005` gave a false freeze from t=0 on a sparse scene); duration against the voice-over;
determinism hash with `ffmpeg -f framemd5` across two renders; a vision-model critic on the contact
sheet and 3 to 5 key frames, at most 2 repair passes. Layout is the dominant failure of agent-written
Manim, and a z-order bug (text hidden under an opaque panel) passes any bounds audit: look at the frames.

Pin and test CLI flags against the installed version. On CE 0.22.0 these documented flags fail:
`--write_to_movie`, `--flush_cache`, `-i`, and `-o` with more than one scene.

Content guard: code makes false statements look exact. Spot-check the 2 or 3 most surprising claims against
a source, and test any simulation against an independent reference.

## Teaching rules from 3Blue1Brown's source

Distilled as ideas; the example code in repositories that adapt 3b1b/videos is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so
vendor none of it. Source counts (text matches over 2023 to 2026 code): the modal move is 2 s
(`run_time=2` is 30% of explicit run times), highlight rectangles and opacity do the pointing (flashes
are rare), emphasis comes from slowing at landings. Scale to a short: 1 to 1.5 s moves with 0.5 to 1 s
holds, 3 to 4 s only for the payoff.

1. The first frame shows the object already doing its job; pose the question as a highlight or a few
   words, not a title card.
2. Concrete instance, then pattern, then symbol. The equation arrives after the picture.
3. Assign a colour to each concept once, write the map down, reuse it for every shape, label and
   equation term, and never reuse a colour for a second concept.
4. Show the naive attempt failing, then the repair.
5. One focal winner per beat at full brightness; the rest at 0.2 to 0.4 opacity.
6. Carry identity: transform an object into its next representation (picture to number to symbol)
   instead of replacing it; demote finished results to a corner instead of deleting them.
7. Narration says why, visuals show what; do not read the screen aloud.
8. A breath of 0.5 to 1 s before the reveal and a longer hold after it.
9. Close the loop: the last frame answers the first frame's question and is the longest hold.

For 9:16 the "pause and ponder" of long lessons becomes a visible beat of silence or an on-screen
question. The strongest maths Shorts observed are single-question puzzles with a visual hook, not
condensed lectures.

## Science families

| Family | Engine | The visual | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| physics (pendulum, orbit, wave) | Manim with a precomputed trajectory, or canvas and Three.js | a dot or vector on a path with 1 to 2 live readouts | integrator drift (above) |
| chemistry and molecules | HTML with Three.js, or Manim chemistry plugin | 2D skeletal formula morphing; 3D ball-and-stick | check structure validity: wrong structures look authoritative |
| biology and cells | SVG plus GSAP, layered labelled diagram | organelles revealed one at a time | Manim is weak at organic shape: use an illustrated SVG asset |
| algorithms | Manim | bars or nodes with colour states (compare yellow, swapped green, done grey) and a synced pseudocode panel | state-colour map must be fixed |
| data structures, neural nets | Manim or HTML boxes and arrows | growing node by node, activations as brightness | plugins without a licence file cannot be reused |
| probability and statistics | Manim for exact areas, HTML for charts | histogram converging, area model | unseeded random is not reproducible |
| finance (compound interest) | HTML, GSAP and d3 scales | curve drawing on, big end number, doubling markers | counters and tick labels need LaTeX in Manim |
| astronomy scale | Three.js or a log-scale 2D camera | log-axis zoom with labelled objects | linear coordinates lose precision across 10^20 |
